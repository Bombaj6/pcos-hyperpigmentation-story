The Triad: How Insulin Resistance Controls Your Entire Body

Over 70% to 80% of women with PCOS suffer from severe cellular insulin resistance—regardless of whether they are considered "lean" or have excess weight.

When your cells become deaf to insulin, your pancreas goes into overdrive, pumping out up to 400% more insulin than normal. Here is exactly what happens next:

Term 1: The Fat-Lock
Hormonal Weight Resistance

1. The "Fat-Lock" Effect (Why 1,200 Calories Fails)

Insulin is not just a blood sugar shuttle—it is the human body's primary fat-storage hormone. High circulating insulin completely blocks lipolysis (fat breakdown). It forcibly shunts every calorie into visceral fat cells around your lower abdomen, while keeping fat stores locked tight. You can starve on 1,000 calories, and your body will burn muscle before it touches your belly fat.

Term 2: Anovulatory Cycles
Missing & Irregular Cycles

2. The Ovary Blockade (Why Periods Vanish For 90+ Days)

Your ovaries have direct insulin receptors. When flooded with excess insulin, the theca cells inside the ovaries are triggered to overproduce male hormones (testosterone and androgens). High androgens arrest egg follicles in an immature state—preventing ovulation. Without ovulation, your body cannot produce progesterone, leaving you stranded for months without a natural period.

Term 3: Glucose Instability
High Blood Sugar & Crashes

3. The Glucose Rollercoaster (The 2:00 PM Crash & Cravings)

Because cells resist insulin, glucose cannot enter muscle tissue to be used as cellular fuel. Your cells are literally starving in a sea of sugar! Your brain triggers emergency hunger signals—causing overwhelming, compulsive cravings for carbs, followed by reactive hypoglycemia (shaky hands, irritability, anxiety, and afternoon brain fog).

⚠️ The Birth Control Trap

Birth control pills do not "fix" irregular periods in PCOS. They induce an artificial chemical withdrawal bleed, masking the condition while worsening underlying insulin resistance and liver stress. When you eventually stop the pill, the missing periods and weight gain return with a vengeance.
